4-Step Problem Solving Protocol & Exam Pitfalls

Step 1: Identify Constants vs. Changing Variables

Before taking any derivative, separate quantities that stay constant throughout the motion (like ladder length L, tank ratio R/H, or radar distance D) from variables that change with time t.

Step 2: Reduce Extra Variables Before Differentiating

In the Conical Tank and Moving Shadow problems, use Similar Triangles to eliminate an unwanted variable (r = (R/H)h or s = [h/(H−h)]x) prior to differentiating so you avoid messy product rules.

Step 3: Differentiate Implicitly with Respect to Time (t)

Apply the Chain Rule and Product Rule to every time-dependent variable: d/dt [x·y] = x(dy/dt) + y(dx/dt) and d/dt [lwh] = wh(dl/dt) + lh(dw/dt) + lw(dh/dt). Never substitute snapshot values before differentiating!

Step 4: Watch Sign Conventions & Units

Decreasing quantities have negative derivatives (dV/dt < 0 when draining, dy/dt < 0 as a ladder slides down). Always include units such as ft/min, cm³/s, or rad/s on AP free-response questions.
