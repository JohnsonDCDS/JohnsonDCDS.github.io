MVT & Differential Derivation
By the Mean Value Theorem, $\frac{\Delta y_i}{\Delta x_i} = f'(c_i)$ for some $c_i \in (x_{i-1}, x_i)$. Thus $\Delta s_i = \sqrt{1 + [f'(c_i)]^2}\,\Delta x_i$. Taking the limit as $n \to \infty$ yields $L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}\,dx$.

Arc Length & Right Triangles Guide

AP Calculus BC Conceptual Derivation

1. Pythagorean Subinterval Approximation

The arc length of a smooth curve $y = f(x)$ over $[a, b]$ is approximated by summing straight line hypotenuses across $n$ subintervals: $\sum_{i=1}^n \Delta s_i$, where each segment $\Delta s_i = \sqrt{(\Delta x_i)^2 + (\Delta y_i)^2}$.

2. Factoring out Δx & Mean Value Theorem

Factoring out $\Delta x$: $\Delta s_i = \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\Delta y_i}{\Delta x_i}\right)^2} \Delta x_i$. By the Mean Value Theorem (MVT), there exists $c_i \in (x_{i-1}, x_i)$ such that $\frac{\Delta y_i}{\Delta x_i} = f'(c_i)$.

3. The Definite Integral Limit

Taking the limit as $n \to \infty$ ($\Delta x \to 0$) converts the Riemann sum into the standard definite integral: $L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}\,dx$.
