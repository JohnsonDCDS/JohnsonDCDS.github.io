Average Value of a Function Guide

AP Calculus BC Conceptual Derivation

1. Definition of Average Value

The average value of a continuous function $f(x)$ on $[a, b]$ is $f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b - a} \int_{a}^{b} f(x)\,dx$. It extends the arithmetic mean of discrete numbers to continuous functions over an interval.

2. Geometric Interpretation ("Equal Area Rectangle")

Multiplying both sides by $(b - a)$ gives $f_{\text{avg}} \cdot (b - a) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$. The area under $y = f(x)$ from $x=a$ to $x=b$ is exactly equal to the area of a rectangle with width $(b-a)$ and height $f_{\text{avg}}$.

3. Mean Value Theorem (MVT) for Integrals

If $f(x)$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, there exists at least one value $c \in (a, b)$ such that $f(c) = f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.

4. "Area Balancing" Concept

The horizontal line $y = f_{\text{avg}}$ acts as a "water level." The area of the region above $y = f_{\text{avg}}$ where $f(x) > f_{\text{avg}}$ (surplus) exactly equals the missing area below $y = f_{\text{avg}}$ where $f(x) < f_{\text{avg}}$ (deficit).
